# Kids · Episode 001 · revision 3 · "Who Keeps Pressing Play?" (working title)

Story-first rewrite per the story/business brief (9 Oct 2026): one relatable interrupted activity, a prediction, Dexter's wrong guess, Hoot's single hint, an investigation the viewer can see, an application and a resolved ending. Narrator: **Dexter** (Brian), first person. Ages 7–9. Claims → `claims.md` (K-numbers). Revision 2 (statistics tower + Scroll Monster fight) is superseded; the card can stay "Scroll Tamer".

**Characters in this episode:** Dexter (curious, inventive, *guesses too quickly*), Professor Hoot (asks "What did you notice?", gives one hint). The recurring peer companion is not used until its design is tested.

## L1 · the problem, already happening (K9)
*(Sunset. A half-built cardboard rocket. Dexter holds a tablet.)*
Hoot and I are building a rocket, and we have to finish before sunset! I'll just watch ONE quick video about rocket fins…
*(Video ends. "Up next" — another starts by itself. Then another.)*
Wait… where did the sun go? Our rocket isn't finished!

## L2 · the question + prediction
Hi, I'm Dexter. And I have a mystery: why couldn't I stop after ONE video? What do you think? Was it because I'm just bad at stopping… or did something keep pressing play? Have a think!

## L3 · the wrong guess (Dexter's flaw)
I know! I just need SUPER willpower! Watch this. One video… and I stop.
*(Dexter stares, fists clenched. The video ends. Another starts by itself. Dexter melts into the chair.)*
Okay. That didn't work.

## L4 · the hint and the investigation (K9)
Professor Hoot: "What did you notice, Dexter… right at the end of the video?"
Let's watch the end again, very closely. Look! A little countdown: "Up next… three, two, one." Nobody pressed play. The app pressed it for me!
That's called autoplay: when one video ends, the next one starts all by itself. Lots of apps are built that way, so it's hard for anyone to stop, even grown-ups. It's not because I'm bad. It's how it's built.

## L5 · the test
Let's test it. Professor Hoot, can you switch autoplay off?
*(Hoot taps a switch. The video ends. The screen just… waits.)*
Ooh. Now when a video ends, nothing happens. I get to choose. And I choose… rocket!

## L6 · applying the idea at bedtime (K6, K7, K10)
And you know when else this matters? Bedtime. Kids our age need 9 to 12 hours of sleep every night, and scientists found that kids who use screens at bedtime often sleep less and feel sleepier the next day.
So tonight I'm setting a timer, and when it rings, my tablet goes to bed first. Goodnight, tablet!

## L7 · resolution, mission, goodbye (one optional app line)
*(Morning. The rocket is finished. Countdown — launch!)*
We did it! So, what did we discover? Videos can keep playing by themselves, but you can be the boss: switch autoplay off with a grown-up, use a timer, and give your screen a bedtime.
Your mission: with a grown-up, find the autoplay switch on one app you use, and decide together if it should be on or off.
Want to read this story again? It's at dexty.live.
Did you know that? Now you do. Bye for now!
