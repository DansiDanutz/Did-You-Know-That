Status key: **verified** = primary or institutional text read · **corroborated** = two or more independent secondary
sources agree, primary not read · **hedged** = historians disagree or the source qualifies it, and the script carries
that hedge · **RE-CHECK** = re-open before voicing · **not used** = checked and left out.

## Viewer prior vs. claim
- **Prior:** "Money is the notes and coins in my wallet, and the number in my banking app is just a record of cash the bank is keeping for me."
- **Claim that breaks it:** most money today isn't anything you can hold. In Britain about 97 of every 100 pounds of
  broad money are bank deposits, and most deposits are created when banks make loans (F1). The Bank of England's own
  definition is that money is a special kind of IOU, trusted because everyone expects others to accept it (F2). Money is
  a promise, and the episode climbs to that answer rung by rung.

## Myths and disputes, and how the script handles each
| Popular claim | What the evidence says | Script handling |
|---|---|---|
| "Money was invented to replace barter." | No society running on barter alone has been documented (Humphrey 1985; Graeber 2011). Barter shows up mostly among people who already know money. | Named and corrected (F7), then closed neutrally. It doesn't claim to know the true origin. |
| "Sweden printed the world's first banknotes." | First in **Europe**. China used paper money centuries earlier. | "the first real banknotes in Europe" (F9). |
| "Banks create money out of thin air, without limit." | The BoE says banks create deposits by lending, but monetary policy, regulation and profitability limit it. | "Within limits, a loan creates new money" (F1). |
| "Nixon took America off the gold standard in 1971." | Ordinary Americans lost gold convertibility in 1933. 1971 ended convertibility for foreign governments. | DYK is the 1933 ban (F20); Nixon line says "foreign governments" (F21). |
| "Money today is backed by nothing." | The BoE says confidence rests on the central bank's monetary policy (price stability). | "A promise backed by trust. Trust in the people who run it." (F21, F16). |
| "The word *millionaire* was coined for John Law's speculators (1719)." | The TLFi's first dated attestation is **1740** (Le Sage). A June 1720 newspaper use is reported on blogs, but I couldn't confirm it in an authoritative dictionary. | **Not used.** |
| "Tulip mania ruined the Dutch economy (1637)." | Anne Goldgar (*Tulipmania*, 2007) found no evidence of mass bankruptcy or economic damage. The famous stories come mostly from moralising pamphlets. | **Not used** (no Dutch era). If David wants it, it works only as a debunk. |
| "The hunchback rented his back as a desk." | Famous, but sourced to Mackay-era retellings. | "The story goes…" (F14). |
| "Bubble share price 500 → 15,000 livres." | Sources disagree. Velde: ~250 → 10,000 (40×). | Velde's numbers, "about forty times" (F13). |
| "Hollowed cacao beans filled with dirt." | The friar's record says sellers counterfeited cacao. The filling details come from popular retellings. | "a Spanish friar wrote about sellers who faked cacao beans" (F6). |
| "M-Pesa lifted 2 % of Kenyan households out of poverty." | Suri & Jack (*Science*, 2016) estimate. Critics (Bateman, Duvendack & Loubere, *Review of African Political Economy* 2019) dispute the methods. | **Not narrated** (optional line in STORY §12, with the hedge "one study estimated… others dispute it"). |

## Candidates checked and NOT used
- **Potosí silver (1545+) and the price revolution:** strong, but the 1500 slot is Tenochtitlan. It would push a second
  Spanish-Americas era and a second "metal" rung. Possible swap for 1500 if David prefers metal over cacao (§12).
- **Amsterdam Wisselbank (1609):** good "bank money" story, but it overlaps the 1661 paper rung, and there's no Dutch slot.
- **Yap stone money (rai):** popular, and its details are often wrong (the "stone at the bottom of the sea" story is
  from Furness 1910, anecdotal). Not needed.
- **"Millionaire" etymology:** see myths. TLFi gives 1740.
- **Law's 1694 escape details** (bribes, a coach to a ship): from a single blog. Not narrated.
- **~300 forgery executions 1797–1821:** numismatic estimate, not primary. Narrated as "many" (F18).
- **Riksbank as "the world's oldest central bank":** widely said, but I didn't need it, so it isn't verified.
- **Cortés's "more than sixty thousand" in the Tenochtitlan/Tlatelolco market (Second Letter, 1520):** real quote, but his
  claim, and the Tlatelolco tie is a later gloss. Not narrated. It could inform the market visual (labelled illustration).
- **Suri & Jack 2016 M-Pesa poverty estimate:** disputed. Not narrated by default.
- **UK cash share of payments (UK Finance):** not fetched. Europe's ECB figure (F24) is used instead.
