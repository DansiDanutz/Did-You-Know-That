# Strategy (adopted from the story/business brief, 9 Oct 2026)

Source: `~/Documents/ChatGPT/Did-You-Know-That/DEXTY-STORY-GAME-BUSINESS-AUDIT.html` and its Claude brief. Figures about YouTube programmes are as stated in that audit — **recheck the official pages before relying on them.**

## The business
- **Primary product: original YouTube stories.** A viewer who enjoys an episode entirely on YouTube is a success; the app never adds friction to "prove conversion".
- **Dexty is the companion:** free discovery cards, sources, independent missions, later recall, sharing. Someone may read and save without watching.
- **Fresh channel, zero audience (confirmed by David).** Budget YouTube ad income at **$0** until Partner Programme eligibility and acceptance are confirmed (audit: full ads route needs 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 qualifying public watch hours in 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days, then review).
- **Made-for-kids uploads** lose personalised ads, comments, notification bells and clickable end screens/cards; the kids plan cannot depend on them.
- **Embedding earns only for our own eligible videos**; it is not a separate payout.
- **Budget rule:** count writing, research, animation, voice, editing, rights, localisation, QA, hosting and human time. Any pilot spend needs a capped, approved budget and a stop/rework decision afterwards.

## Brand hierarchy
- Umbrella: **Did You Know That?** — "Discover the story behind the surprise." Close: "Did you know that? Now you do."
- Kids product: **Dexter's Wonder Adventures** (working name) — "Help Dexter figure it out." The Road of Wonders is the kids world.
- Adults product: **Did You Know That?** documentary stories — "There is a remarkable explanation behind this."
- Long term: distinct kids and adult channel identities when both have sustainable production (each must qualify separately). Do not create or migrate channels without authorisation.

## Launch sequence (no dates promised)
1. Stabilise the app (stage 1 merged; deploy on approval).
2. Two 20–30 s motion tests: a kids acting scene and an adult evidence scene.
3. Finish one kids pilot and one adult pilot (approved claim ledger, script, captions, sources, rights, QA, card, discovery page). Lead with kids.
4. Small formative test with consenting families/adults: comprehension, memory, interest, comfortable stopping.
5. Two follow-up stories for the stronger, repeatable series; measure production time and cost.
6. Publish with authorisation and an approved buffer; no daily promise. A daily *curated* discovery from the approved catalogue is fine if resurfaced items are labelled.
7. Review the first 6–8 episodes (titles, thumbnails, opening retention, clarity, cost) before scaling.

## Effort split for the next cycle (proposal, not a budget)
50 % research/script/animation pilots · 25 % app reliability and key journeys · 15 % audience testing/packaging · 10 % measurement/operations.

## Scorecard
| Perspective | Measure | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Viewer | First-minute retention, average duration, retention dips | Proof of learning |
| Story | Unaided retelling of question and answer | Fast cuts, loud effects |
| Learning | Immediate and delayed explanation, application | Saved cards, video completion |
| Parent | Understands age fit, sources, YouTube behaviour, endings | A "must-have" claim |
| Business | Cost/time per episode, catalogue views, actual eligible revenue | CPM × every play, installs |
| App | Watch entry, save/revisit, recovery, crashes/storage failures | Time in app |

Set numerical targets only after a baseline. No new child tracking without a privacy review.
