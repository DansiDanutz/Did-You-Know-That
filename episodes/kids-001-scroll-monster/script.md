# "The Scroll Monster" · revision 2 · narration script

Narrator: **Dexter** (ElevenLabs Brian), first person, to the viewer. Ages 7–9. Claim references → `claims.md`. Line ids become `video/ep01-kids/vo-lines.json` entries when recorded.

## L1 · problem (claim K9)
Uh-oh. One video ends… and the next one starts all by itself. And again. And again. Why is it SO hard to stop?

## L2 · mission
Hi! I'm Dexter, the brightest lightbulb on the Road of Wonders. My mission today: find out why screens are so hard to stop, and how YOU can be the boss of them. Are you in? Let's go!

## L3 · discovery: the Screen-Time Tower (K1, K2, K3, K4, K5)
First, a clue! Researchers in America asked more than a thousand kids how long they spend on screens just for fun. In 2021, kids aged 8 to 12 said about five and a half hours a day! Teenagers? Almost nine hours. That's more than a whole school day! And in a newer study, little kids aged 2 to 4 spent about two hours a day.
Quick, make a guess: how many hours is it for you? Hold up your fingers!
Hmm. That's a lot of hours. But WHY?

## L4 · complication: test a wrong idea, kindly (K9)
Maybe kids just aren't trying hard enough to stop? Let's test it! Professor Hoot, you're a very wise owl. Watch one video, then stop.
*(Hoot watches; the next video starts by itself; "just one more…" three times.)*
Whoa! Even Professor Hoot got stuck! So it's not just you. Lots of apps are built so that when one video ends, the next one starts all by itself. It's called autoplay. It's a trick… and here comes the one who loves it most: the Scroll Monster!

## L5 · payoff: beat the monster (K6, K7, K10)
The Scroll Monster is sneaky. He grabs dinner: "just one more video…". He grabs sleep! Kids your age need 9 to 12 hours of sleep every night, and scientists found that kids who use screens at bedtime often sleep less and feel sleepier the next day.
But now we know his trick, so we can beat him! Power one: a timer. When it rings, YOU decide to stop. Power two: ask a grown-up to switch autoplay off. Power three: give your screen a bedtime, before yours!
Ta-da! The Scroll Monster shrinks right back into the phone.

## L6 · little siblings (K8)
And if you have a little brother or sister aged 2 to 4, health experts at the World Health Organization say no more than one hour of screens a day for them. Less is even better!

## L7 · recap, family mission, goodbye (one optional app line)
So, explorer, what did we discover? Screens are built to be hard to stop, but you can be the boss!
Your mission for today: with a grown-up, choose a bedtime for your screen and put it to bed, maybe in a cosy basket.
Want to read this story again and keep its card? It's waiting for you at dexty.live.
Great job today, explorer. Bye for now!
