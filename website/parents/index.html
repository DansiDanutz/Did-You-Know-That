Did You Know That?

For parents and carers

What Dexty is, and what it keeps

Short true stories with Dexter: read or listen, try a small experiment, and keep a card of what you discovered. No account, no ads, no streaks.

Who it is for

Kids mode is written first for ages 7–9 and is labelled 6–12 in the app. Each story asks one question, lets your child guess, then explains the answer with a picture or a small experiment. Teens & Adults mode tells documentary-style stories for older readers.

The goal of each story is understanding one idea well enough to explain it or use it on a new example. A saved card means "I found this story"; it is not proof that something was learned.

Where the facts come from

Each episode is written from a list of claims, and each claim is checked against sources that were opened and read. The sources are listed on the episode's public page. Simplifications for children are marked as simplifications, and fantasy props (like Dexter's prism lamp) are presented as make-believe.

YouTube

Watching is always optional. Every story can be read or listened to in the app, and nothing in the game requires a video.

Nothing in the game rewards watching, liking, subscribing or sharing: cards, outfits and missions never depend on it.

The YouTube player loads from youtube.com only when someone presses Watch. From then on YouTube's own terms and privacy policy apply inside the player. No episode is published on YouTube yet.

What stays on this device

Dexty has no accounts and no analytics, and it shows no ads. Everything below is kept in this browser's storage on this device only:

  • saved cards and the coins found on the road;
  • which optional questions were tried (not shown as a score);
  • Dexter's outfits, equipment and workshop from missions;
  • settings: language, Kids or Adults, sound, voice, text size;
  • the optional first name or nickname your child gives Dexter;
  • how many visits there have been, so Dexter can say "welcome back".

The name. The name your child types is never uploaded. Dexter says the name out loud only if it is one of about 600 common first names that were recorded in advance. In that case the app downloads that one recording from dexty.live. Like any website, our host can see which file was downloaded, and so which listed name it is, but not who typed it. Other names appear in text only.

What the app downloads

  • dexty.live: the app itself, pictures and narration recordings. As with any website, the host receives the device's IP address and the files requested.
  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com): the lettering. Google receives the device's IP address.
  • YouTube: only after someone presses Watch.

Dexter's voice was recorded in advance with a voice service (ElevenLabs). The app never contacts it, and it never sees anything your child types.

An early test version had a leaderboard with made-up nicknames. It is switched off: nothing new can be added and the stored nicknames and scores are no longer shown anywhere, though they still exist on our server.

Back up, move or delete

  • Back up / move: Settings (⚙️) → Your progress → Save a backup file. On the other device, choose Restore from a file.
  • Delete everything: clear this site's data in your browser settings, or uninstall the installed app. Without a backup file this cannot be undone.
  • Private windows keep nothing after they close, and the app says so.

Stopping is always fine

There are no streaks, timers, energy bars or random prizes. Missions show the reward before your child starts, and hints never make the reward smaller. When the app closes, Dexter rests safely at camp: nothing is lost and nothing gets worse. Next time he simply says welcome back.

Things to do together

  • Shadow watch (after The Missing Shadow): on a sunny day, push a stick into the ground and mark the end of its shadow in the morning and again in the afternoon. What changed, and why?
  • Ask your child to explain a saved card to you in their own words. Then ask: "How could we check that?"
