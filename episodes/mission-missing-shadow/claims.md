# Claim ledger: "The Missing Shadow" (Did You Know That? app mission, Dexter, ages 7–9)

- **Date checked:** 2026-10-09
- **Method:** Every source below was opened in this session. Web pages were read with WebFetch. PDFs were downloaded (curl, or the WebFetch-saved binary) and read with `pdftotext -layout`. The University of York BEST `.doc` was downloaded with curl and converted with macOS `textutil`. Each table row names its tool. Quotes are kept to about 12 words or fewer. Anything longer is paraphrased, with its location given. Claims that no opened source supports are marked UNVERIFIED. Nothing is cited from memory.
- **Verdict key:** VERIFIED means an opened authoritative source states the claim and the kid wording stays inside it. NEEDS CARE means the claim is true only with conditions, or the wording has to be narrowed. UNVERIFIED means no opened source supports it.

## Misconceptions to address in feedback

1. **"The shadow is a reflection, or Dexter's copy, with his face on it."** The shadow has only an outline and no details, because it is just light that got blocked (S10a, BEST/York; Beech Hill).
2. **"Shadows are made of darkness or stuff."** A shadow is a lack of direct light (S10b, BEST answer A). Don't present this as a research finding (source not opened).
3. **"The Sun goes around the Earth" / "the Sun moves across the sky."** Always say *looks like* or *seems to*. The Earth spins (S6: UK NC Year 5, NASA Changing Shadows).
4. **"The shade moved because the tree moved."** The tree stays still and the Earth turns (S10c, correction from S6).
5. **"Shadows are always the same size and shape."** The size changes with the light's height and distance. The outline matches, but the shadow can stretch (S2, S4; UK NC Year 3 "size of shadows change").
6. **"Only the Sun makes shadows."** Torches and lamps make them too (Beech Hill; Bear's Shadow, "Optional extensions": the Sun, light bulbs and flashlights are all light sources).
7. **"Shortest shadow = 12 o'clock" / "shadows vanish at noon."** The shortest shadow comes at *solar* noon, which is often not 12:00 on the clock. Shadows vanish only where the Sun can be straight overhead (the tropics) (S7).
8. **"Sunglasses make it OK to look at the Sun."** False (S9: NASA; UK NC).

**Wording to avoid:** "the Sun moves/travels across the sky" (without "looks like"); "the Sun goes around the Earth"; "shadows are shortest at 12 o'clock"; "noon shadows point north" (without naming the hemisphere); "your shadow disappears at noon" (true only in the tropics, sometimes); "a shadow is exactly the same as the object"; "shadows are made of darkness"; "a sundial tells clock time"; "line the sundial up with a compass" (not verified); "wear sunglasses to look at the Sun".
