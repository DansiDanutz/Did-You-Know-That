## 1. Brief
- **The question (0–8 s):** Why is it so hard to stop watching when one video ends and the next starts by itself?
- **Why it matters to a kid:** it happens to them every day; it can steal sleep, dinner and play.
- **What they understand at the end:** screens are *designed* to be hard to stop — it's not because you're bad — and you can be the boss with a timer, autoplay off and a screen bedtime.
- **Words explained in context:** researchers, autoplay, screen time.
- **Real-world mission (end):** with a grown-up, choose a bedtime for your screen and "put it to bed".
- **Must not:** shame kids or families, scare, say all screens are bad, present the "brain buzzing" image as science, claim autoplay defaults, cite unverified AAP limits.
- **Cast:** Dexter (narrator), Professor Hoot (gets stuck too — shows it isn't the child's fault), the Scroll Monster (goofy, never scary).
- **Changes from the current master (revision 1, 298.5 s):**
  1. Opens with the problem, not the app; Dexter's introduction follows in one line.
  2. Statistics corrected to the ledger (2021 tweens/teens with the year; little kids from the 2024 data; "researchers", not "scientists").
  3. New complication beat: "maybe kids just aren't trying hard enough?" — tested kindly (Hoot gets stuck too).
  4. Sleep line reworded to the evidence (association); WHO limit stated for ages 2–4.
  5. Removed: the neon magic-word card, "3 videos a day", the five-step "win the card" ending, the XP/hearts HUD and the fake clickable end screen (made-for-kids videos have no working end-screen elements).
  6. Ends with a recap, a family mission and one optional app line.
