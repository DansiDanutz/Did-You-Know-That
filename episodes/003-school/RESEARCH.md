Every narrated fact and every on-screen number in `STORY.md` traces to an F-number here. Researched 2026-10-13 with
web search plus primary text where it was reachable (Perseus LSJ, the GHDI translation of the 1763 Prussian
regulation, the UN UDHR page, the UNESCO 2021 report PDF, the World Bank blog). Story Council was **not** run (it's a

Status key: **verified** = primary or institutional text read · **corroborated** = two or more independent secondary
sources agree, primary not read · **hedged** = historians disagree or the source qualifies it, and the script carries
that hedge · **not used** = checked and left out.

## Viewer prior vs. claim
- **Prior:** "School is just what kids do. It's always been there, and it's always been a chore."
- **Claim that breaks it:** for most of history, school was for almost nobody. The word itself first meant *free time*
  (F1). In about 200 years, the world went from roughly 1 in 10 adults who could read to almost 9 in 10 (F3).

## Myths and disputes, and how the script handles each
| Popular claim | What the evidence says | Script handling |
|---|---|---|
| "The Prussian system was designed to make obedient factory workers." | Obedience and social discipline were real aims (Melton). Factories weren't: 1763 Prussia was agrarian, and the "factory" framing is mostly Gatto's later polemic. Enforcement was patchy. | Named and corrected in plain words (F12): "not that simple… a land of farms… loyal, God-fearing, disciplined subjects… plenty of families ignored the rules." |
| "The Old Deluder Satan Act was named after the devil." | It's a nickname from its first words. | "begins by blaming the devil" (F9). |
| "One Lancaster teacher taught 1,000 children." | Lancaster's own promotional claim. | "claimed" (F13). |
| "Tutoring makes you better than 98 % of classmates (2 sigma)." | Bloom's 1984 figure, from small studies mixed with mastery learning. Modern meta-analyses find ~0.3–0.8 SD. | Bloom attributed + "later studies found the effect is smaller. But it's real." (F21). |
| "AI tutoring gave Nigerian kids two years of learning in six weeks." | It's the World Bank's own conversion of a ~0.3 SD effect in a short, teacher-guided pilot. | "the World Bank estimated nearly two years" + "One small study. Short." (F22). |
| "School summer holidays exist because of farm work." | Contested, and US-centric (rural children often attended in summer; urban calendars drove the long break). | **Not used.** The 1763 "no holidays even at harvest" clause (F10) is the verified alternative. |
| "Victorian schools were free." | Fees ("school pence") were charged until the 1891 fee grant. | "In 1891, for most families, it becomes free" (F14). |
| "*Leisure* comes from *scholé*." | False (Old French *leisir*). | Never said (F1). |

## Candidates checked and NOT used
- **"In 1870 only one in four people in the world attended school"** (OWID literacy page): the wording is ambiguous
  (share of population vs. children). Not narrated.
- **"754 million adults can't read"**: no matching current source. UNESCO's latest figure is **765 million adults
  lack basic literacy** (International Literacy Day 2024, via UK National Commission for UNESCO and bpb.de). Usable
  if David wants it, but it isn't in the script.
- **Prussia's 1717 edict (Frederick William I)**: real, but it isn't needed and I couldn't reach a primary text.
