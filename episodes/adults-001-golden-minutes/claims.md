**Method.** Each narration claim (A1 to A11) was checked against sources opened in this session. Most were opened with WebFetch. Where WebFetch was blocked (Guardian, BBC, Telegraph, state.gov, datareportal full report), the page was downloaded with `curl` (or from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine) and its text searched directly. PDFs were downloaded and converted with `pdftotext`. Sources that could not be opened are listed as **not opened** and are never cited as support. Quotations are 12 words or fewer; everything else is paraphrased with a location. Search-engine summaries were used only to find URLs and are not cited.

Verdict key: **VERIFIED** (the script may keep this, with the attribution shown) · **NEEDS REWORDING** (the substance is supported, but the wording, numbers or attribution are wrong) · **UNVERIFIED** (no source opened supports it) · **REMOVE** (not a factual claim, or one that cannot be defended).

## Changes needed in the script

1. **A2 (most important):** Do not present "200,000 human lifetimes every day" as fact. The figure first appears in the Telegraph (2019), not the BBC or The Times (2018). No method was ever published, and as a literal daily total it would require about 22 extra hours per internet user per day. Either drop the number or flag it on air as a rough, disputed estimate.
2. **A4:** Brichter did not say the slot-machine/lever line; The Guardian's journalist wrote it. Use Brichter's real words: "I agree 100%" (to the slot-machine comparison) and "Pull-to-refresh is addictive." His own comparison is the elevator "close door" button.
3. **A7:** Remove "untouched". After 100 years, most of each fund was spent on public works (Boston paid out in 1894), and only the remainder compounded to 1990. Use Boston ≈ $4.5M (the contemporaneous 1990 report) rather than $5M. For Philadelphia, say about $2M in total, of which the city kept about $520k after the state's share. The reason for the gap, per the sources: different management, and both cities lending less than Franklin intended.
4. **A9:** Update the FSI figures to the current 552–690 class hours (Spanish and French: 30 weeks, 690 h), since 600–750 h is the pre-2021 wording. Say "classroom hours plus homework", and keep "about 3–4 years at 30 min/day" only as an illustration. Better: "almost four years".
5. **A5:** Replace "strongest habit-builders psychology has ever measured" with: "In lab studies, unpredictable rewards keep behaviour going longer than predictable ones."
6. **A6:** Replace "now" with "in early 2025" and credit GWI via DataReportal's Digital 2025 report (adult internet users, self-reported).
7. **A1:** Change "invented" to "designed" or "is widely credited with", because others have claimed credit.
8. **A3:** Add "in 2009" and "Tweetie, a Twitter app that Twitter later bought".
9. **A8:** Optionally add "in one experiment"; the numbers are from Experiment 2 (SSSS vs STTT), and the 5-minute score was 83% vs 71%.
10. **A10:** Say "classroom hours" and optionally add Cantonese.
11. **A11:** Delete the leaderboard and "stories you won't hear anywhere else" lines.
